# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

In the spirit of the **Kula-Ring**, **Bhoodan**, and the quiet generosity of **Amélie Poulain**, we as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free, welcoming, and empowering experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

---

## The Amélie Conduct Invariants

In addition to standard community expectations, Amélie operates under specific ethical principles unique to a gift-economy:

### 1. The Zero-Pressure Invariant (No Guilt, No Obligation)
When gifting an idea to an organization, researcher, or maintainer:
- **Never follow up aggressively or demand a reply.**
- **Never guilt-trip a recipient** for not building the idea or not acknowledging your message.
- A gift given with strings attached or expectations of social gratitude is not a gift—it is an emotional invoice. Amélie contributors give once and walk away.

### 2. Respect for Developer & Maintainer Attention
Open-source maintainers and civic researchers are frequently exhausted and under-resourced:
- We **do not spam** maintainers with raw, unvetted prompts.
- We do not suggest enormous architectural changes to solo open-source projects unless we are bringing working code or an implementation grant.
- Every communication must be concise, researched, and respectful of the recipient's finite time.

### 3. Radical Humility in Ideation
- Having an idea is the easy part. Building, maintaining, documenting, and sustaining software in production is where 99% of the real effort lies.
- We never patronize builders with "I have the vision, you just do the coding." We provide blueprints as humble offerings, acknowledging that the builder does the heavy lifting.

### 4. Public Domain & Anti-Enclosure
- Contributors must never attempt to retroactively patent, trademark, copyright, or enclose ideas contributed to Amélie.
- Any attempt to use Amélie to front-run patent claims or troll builders will result in immediate and permanent banishment.

## Standards of Behavior

**Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:**
* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes.
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the wider public good.

**Examples of unacceptable behavior:**
* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances.
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
* Public or private harassment.
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission.
* Spamming recipient organizations or violating the one-touch outreach protocol.
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional or ethical open-source setting.

---

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces (GitHub repositories, issue trackers, discussions, chat rooms, community calls), and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces or contacting idea recipients on behalf of Amélie.

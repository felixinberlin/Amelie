# Amélie — Search Playbook

How step 0.5 (checking) works, and what each round has improved about it.
**Read at the start of every idea round. Add to the "Retro" section at the end of every round.**

Claude doesn't learn between chats. This document and `amelie-pruefprotokoll.md` are the memory. What isn't written here gets repeated — mistakes included.

---

## 1. Order per idea (max. 4 searches, then verdict)

| # | Search | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`<likely recipient> AI`** (blog, project page) | The cheapest kill. In Round 2, 3 of 12 recipients had already built the idea themselves (Wheelmap, CompGen, Repair Café). Also spares you the worst possible first contact. |
| 2 | **German, functional words** — what the thing *does*, not what it's called | Finds associations, agencies, forums |
| 3 | **English, product words** (`app`, `AI`, `tool`, year) | Finds commercial products that don't show up in German (SATB separation) |
| 4 | **Forum / niche** (Akkudoktor, Restarters, Discourse, GitHub) | Indie apps under the SEO radar (Horisol, July 2026) |

**Stop rule:** one hit that fully covers the idea and is ≤ 12 months old → verdict `occupied`, no further searching. Don't go looking for a reason to save the idea anyway.

**Narrow instead of discard** when the hit has a *named weakness* (e.g. CompGen: the AI hallucinates on weathered stone). The weakness becomes the new idea.

## 2. Search recipes that have worked

**To find open gaps (not just to check ideas):**

- **An official assessment scheme with no digital tool** → `<topic> assessment procedure point system guideline`. Found: the LAG-VSW bird-strike scoring scheme.
- **A bottleneck named by the organization itself** → `<project> evaluation takes` / `up to … minutes` / `evaluated by volunteers`. Found: Thünen, up to 90 minutes per nest box.
- **Workshop reports on AI experiments** → `<association> workshop report AI` / `experiences with AI`. The limits named there are ready-made ideas.
- **Research with no product** → `<technique> smartphone` on arXiv/ECCV workshops; then check whether a tool for laypeople exists.

**What hasn't worked:**

- `<topic> AI accessible app` in German → content farms (bau.de clones). Verdict from this: **unclear**, not "open." Re-search with organization names.
- Absence of hits after a single search is not a finding.

**Rule of thumb, now backed by two rounds of evidence:** if end users would pay for it, or a city could turn it into a press release, it already exists. What's open is what an expert body publishes as a PDF and nobody has ever turned into software.

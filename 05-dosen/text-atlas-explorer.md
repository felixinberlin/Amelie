# Dossier: Text Atlas Explorer

**Problem:** Public institutions, such as the German Research Foundation (DFG) or Berlin's Senate Administrations, face the challenge of overseeing immense volumes of unstructured text data – from research proposals and scientific publications to citizen participation feedback and policy strategy papers – and extracting well-founded insights. Manual analysis is time-consuming, error-prone, and often incomplete. There is a lack of an intuitive tool that not only summarizes but makes the *structure* and *relationships* within these knowledge landscapes visible.

**Solution:** The "Text Atlas Explorer" is a browser-native tool based on local Large Language Models (LLMs) designed to generate interactive knowledge graphs from arbitrary text documents (e.g., PDFs, text files). Instead of summarizing texts, the tool identifies key concepts, entities, arguments, and their relationships, visualizing them in a dynamic, explorative graph. Users can click on nodes to view relevant text passages, filter by theme, or trace chains of argumentation. This enables rapid orientation and deeper insight into complex knowledge domains without sensitive data ever leaving the browser.

**Use Cases:**
*   **DFG:** Analysis of research proposals to identify research gaps, synergies, and emerging trends.
*   **Senate Administration:** Structuring and evaluating citizen comments on urban development projects to recognize recurring concerns and areas of conflict.
*   **Universities/Research Institutes:** Support for literature reviews and mapping research landscapes.

**Benefits:**
*   **Data Privacy:** All AI computations occur locally in the browser, protecting sensitive data.
*   **Interactivity & Visualization:** A playful and intuitive user interface makes knowledge discovery accessible and efficient.
*   **Deeper Insight:** Goes beyond mere keyword search or summaries by revealing structural relationships.
*   **Open Source & CC0:** Full transparency and free use for all public institutions.

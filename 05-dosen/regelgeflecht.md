# Dose: RegelGeflecht (Rule Weave)

## The Friction: Navigating the Bureaucratic Labyrinth

Citizens, small businesses, and even public servants in Europe constantly grapple with the dense, often impenetrable language of administrative regulations, building codes, environmental guidelines, and public policy documents. These texts, while legally precise, present an enormous cognitive barrier. The friction isn't just about finding the right document, but understanding its implications, dependencies, and exceptions without a legal degree. This leads to delays, misunderstandings, non-compliance, and a general disengagement from civic processes. The enforcement gap here is that the *intent* and *spirit* of public regulations are obscured by their form, hindering effective application and citizen empowerment.

## The Asymmetric Inversion: From Text Walls to Interactive Knowledge Landscapes

Instead of forcing users to passively consume static, complex legal documents, "RegelGeflecht" inverts this dynamic. It transforms these documents into an interactive, explorable knowledge graph, running locally in the user's browser. Users upload official PDFs or structured text, and the tool semantically analyzes them to identify key concepts, definitions, obligations, rights, and cross-references. These elements are then visualized as an interconnected graph, where users can click on any node to get a plain-language summary, relevant examples, and direct links back to the original source text. The "enforcement gap" of inaccessible knowledge is closed by proactively providing context and clarity.

## The Amélie Edge: Local-First Empowerment & Civic Delight

"RegelGeflecht" leverages the power of local-first AI (e.g., WebLLM, Onnx Runtime Web) to ensure privacy and rapid feedback, processing sensitive public documents without sending them to external servers. Its core value lies in making civic knowledge delightful and empowering. Imagine a visual interface where complex building permits are rendered as a navigable map of requirements, or environmental regulations as a clear chain of cause and effect. This goes beyond mere summarization; it's about building a semantic understanding layer for public information, fostering civic engagement and reducing bureaucratic overhead for everyone.

## Target Beneficiary

*   **Senatsverwaltung für Stadtentwicklung, Bauen und Wohnen (Berlin)**: For making building codes and urban planning documents more accessible to citizens, architects, and developers.
*   **Bund für Umwelt und Naturschutz Deutschland (BUND)**: To help activists and concerned citizens understand environmental impact assessments and conservation laws.
*   **Universitäten/Research Institutions (e.g., DFG)**: For clarifying complex grant guidelines, ethics protocols, or compliance documents for researchers.

# Dossier: Regulatory Tracery
This Amélie tool addresses the challenge of understanding the complex, multi-layered urban development plans (e.g., zoning plans, land use plans) of Berlin, which have evolved over decades, in terms of their dependencies and historical development. It goes beyond mere text search, aiming to visually represent the "genealogy" of a planning document: Which earlier plans or legal passages were referenced? What changes were made over time, and what consequential effects did they have?

By employing local, WASM-based AI models, the tool can identify references and structural relationships within the documents and create an interactive knowledge graph visualization. Users can thus "explore" the evolution of an urban area or the history of a specific regulation without having to sift through hundreds of PDFs and cross-references. This increases transparency for citizens and accelerates onboarding for administrative staff. The playful exploration of complex interconnections is paramount, with a focus on fast, local processing and appealing visual presentation.

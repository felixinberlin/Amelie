# Dossier: Acoustic Atlas Berlin: Soundscape Profiler

## The Problem
Urban noise pollution is a significant public health and quality of life issue in Berlin. The Senate Department and district offices face challenges in precisely identifying noise sources and implementing effective measures. Current noise maps often rely on modeling, sparse stationary sensor networks, or subjective citizen complaints, which rarely include a detailed classification of noise types (e.g., traffic, construction, human speech, natural sounds). This leads to a lack of granular, actionable data to effectively combat noise hotspots or evaluate the impact of urban development projects. Enforcement of noise protection regulations is hindered by the absence of verifiable, contextualized data.

## The Asymmetric Inversion
We propose a browser-native, AI-powered 'Acoustic Atlas' that overcomes the asymmetry between citizens' subjective perception of noise and the authorities' need for objective, classified data. Instead of merely recording decibel levels, the tool uses local, browser-running AI models (WASM/WebGPU) for real-time classification of ambient sounds. Citizens can record short, anonymized audio snippets, which are analyzed and categorized locally (e.g., 'heavy traffic', 'birdsong', 'construction site', 'human voices'). Only the classified, aggregated, and anonymized metadata (timestamp, location, noise type, intensity) are, with explicit consent, transmitted to the city. This transforms subjective 'complaints' into objective, verifiable, and crucially, *classified* data points.

## The Amélie Solution
The **Acoustic Atlas Berlin** is a web application that utilizes the user's microphone to capture ambient sounds. A lightweight neural network, based on WASM/WebGPU, classifies these sounds into predefined categories (e.g., 'aircraft noise', 'road traffic', 'rail traffic', 'industry', 'construction site', 'human speech', 'nature'). All processing occurs entirely locally in the browser to protect privacy. Users can visualize the classification, track the 'soundscape' of their environment over time, and (optionally) send anonymized, aggregated data points to a central map accessible by the Senate Department. The application is designed to be playful, encouraging participation and raising awareness of one's acoustic environment. It empowers citizens to actively contribute to improving their city and provides authorities with an entirely new data foundation for informed decision-making.

## Target Institution
The **Senatsverwaltung für Umwelt, Mobilität, Verbraucher- und Klimaschutz** (Senate Department for Environment, Mobility, Consumer and Climate Protection) in Berlin is the primary recipient of this data. **Bezirksämter** (district offices) could also directly benefit from local sound profiles to plan and implement noise protection measures at a local level. In the long term, it could also be of interest to environmental organizations like BUND Berlin.
